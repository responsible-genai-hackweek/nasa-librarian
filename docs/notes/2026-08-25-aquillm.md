# AquiLLM — tacit knowledge in research groups

**Research note · 2026-08-25**

[AquiLLM](https://aquillm.org) · [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05648) ·
[repo](https://github.com/AquiLLM/AquiLLM) (pushed 2026-08-25, GPL-2.0, Django/React)

Chandler Campbell, **Bernie Boscoe** (Southern Oregon University) and **Tuan Do** (UCLA).
Funded by Sloan and NSF; deployments described at SOU, UCLA and Cornell.

## What it is

An open-source, open-weight RAG system built for research groups, designed for local or
pseudo-local deployment so groups keep control of their data and models. From the
abstract:

> Although structured data intended for analysis and publication is often well managed,
> much of a group's collective knowledge remains informal, fragmented, or undocumented —
> often passed down orally through meetings, mentoring, and day-to-day collaboration.
> This includes private resources such as emails, meeting notes, training materials, and
> ad hoc documentation. Together, these reflect the group's **tacit knowledge** — the
> informal, experience-based expertise that underlies much of their work.

Ingests almost anything: PDFs, arXiv by ID, VTT/SRT transcripts, scraped webpages,
handwritten notes via OCR, audio/video transcription, spreadsheets, zip batches.
Organised into Collections, queried by chat with source references. Its distinguishing
concern is **privacy** — most RAG systems assume public documents.

## Relevance to us: high, and precise

### 1. They named our problem properly

"Tacit knowledge" is the academic term for what we keep describing as *"lives in ATBDs,
DAAC guides, and in people who have used a product for a decade."* Polanyi's term, with
a real literature behind it (Boscoe's lineage runs through UCLA data-curation and
knowledge-infrastructures work).

**Adopt the term.** It gives our framing citable standing and makes the project legible
to the science-studies half of a workshop like this one.

### 2. Same problem, opposite capture strategy — this is the useful contrast

|  | **AquiLLM** | **Readiness record** |
|---|---|---|
| Captures tacit knowledge as | documents in a retrieval index | typed fields in a schema |
| Ingest cost | very low — point it at a folder | high — someone must author each field |
| Preserves nuance | yes, verbatim | no, lossy by design |
| Answers arbitrary questions | yes | only what the schema anticipated |
| **Can compute a comparison** | **no** | **yes** |
| Trust posture | prose into context | typed values, reviewed |

**The crux:** RAG can retrieve the paragraph saying *"above 80°N, multiple overpasses can
be gridded into a single MGRS tile."* It cannot compare 80°N to your bounding box.
Retrieval surfaces; it does not compute. Our ten fitness axes are **comparisons, not
lookups**, which is why a record and not an index.

The converse is equally true and worth being honest about: a schema holds only what we
thought to define. *"Why did the 2019 reprocessing change my results?"* has no field and
never will.

### 3. They compose — AquiLLM fits the authored tier

Recall the three-tier production model borrowed from SlideRule:

```
authored/    (human-edited)   ─┐
                               ├─▶  merged/  ─▶  the readiness record
generated/   (tool-emitted)   ─┘
```

`generated/` comes from CMR and STAC. **`authored/` is exactly where AquiLLM-shaped
retrieval belongs** — a person (or an extraction pass) writing `cautions[]` for HLSL30
must read ATBDs, DAAC guides, and possibly group correspondence. That is AquiLLM's job
description.

So it is a **candidate tool for producing our authored tier**, not a competitor. The
record is the distillation of the computable subset of what such an index holds.

### 4. Opposite distribution intent — and ours is the more ambitious claim

AquiLLM is deliberately **per-group and private**; tacit knowledge stays in the lab.
Our proposal runs the other way: get dataset-level tacit knowledge **out** of individual
groups and **into the catalog**, published back to DAACs via `geocr:recordEndpoint`.

Same source material, opposite direction of travel. Fitness knowledge about HLSL30
should not be locked in one lab's index — the whole point is that the next lab does not
have to re-derive it.

### 5. A trifecta caution if anyone proposes merging them

AquiLLM's privacy design exists because it ingests **private** group material — leg one
of Jason's lethal trifecta. Our extraction concern was leg three, **untrusted** DAAC
prose. Point one index at both and you have private data and untrusted content in the
same retrievable context, with an agent that can reach the network. Keep the corpora
separate.

## The honest limit on both

Polanyi's original point is that tacit knowledge is partly **not articulable** — *we know
more than we can tell.* A schema captures the articulable-and-comparable part. RAG
captures the articulated-but-unstructured part. Neither captures the genuinely tacit
residue, which is why the expert still has a job.

That is worth saying out loud: it is the honest version of *"we are not replacing them."*

## Actions

- Adopt **"tacit knowledge"** in the framing, citing Campbell, Boscoe & Do 2025.
- Record the RAG-vs-schema choice as an explicit decision (**D10**) rather than an
  implicit one.
- Evaluate AquiLLM as the authoring surface for the `authored/` tier before building any
  document-ingest tooling ourselves.
- Boscoe and Do are worth contacting — the tacit-knowledge framing is theirs, and a
  dataset-level record is a natural extension of it beyond the group boundary.
